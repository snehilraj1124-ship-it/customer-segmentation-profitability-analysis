# Customer Segmentation & Profitability Analysis

## 📊 Project Overview

This project presents a comprehensive **Customer Segmentation & Profitability Analysis** designed to understand customer behavior, identify high-value customer groups, evaluate customer-level profitability, and develop targeted retention and growth strategies.

The analysis uses a **synthetic dataset of 6,000 customers** and connects customer behavior with financial contribution to answer a key business question:

> Which customers create the most sustainable economic value, and how should the business treat each customer segment differently?

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

The project aims to:

- Segment customers based on value and behavior
- Perform RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) analysis
- Analyze revenue contribution by customer segment
- Measure customer-level profitability
- Compare profitable and unprofitable customers
- Evaluate acquisition-channel economics
- Develop a Customer Lifetime Value framework
- Analyze Average Order Value and purchase frequency
- Evaluate discount sensitivity
- Identify category affinities
- Develop customer retention strategies
- Design win-back strategies
- Analyze customer economics by city
- Identify profitability risks
- Develop a 90-day management action plan
- Build a customer profitability KPI framework

---

## 👥 Customer Segmentation Framework

Customers are classified into four major value segments:

### 1. VIP

High-value customers with strong spending, purchase frequency and recent activity.

**Recommended strategy:**
- Personalized recommendations
- Premium service
- Loyalty benefits
- Early access
- Cross-selling
- Personalized retention campaigns

### 2. High Value

Customers with strong economic potential who can be developed into VIP customers.

**Recommended strategy:**
- Cross-selling
- Product bundles
- Loyalty programs
- Subscription offers
- Category expansion

### 3. Emerging

Customers showing potential but requiring increased purchase frequency and engagement.

**Recommended strategy:**
- Second-category adoption
- Frequency incentives
- Personalized recommendations
- Educational campaigns

### 4. At Risk / Low Value

Customers with lower modeled value or weaker recent activity.

**Recommended strategy:**
- Automated win-back
- Low-cost CRM campaigns
- Replenishment reminders
- Selective promotional offers

---

## 🔍 RFM Analysis

The project uses **RFM analysis** to evaluate customers across three dimensions:

### Recency
How recently the customer purchased.

### Frequency
How frequently the customer purchases.

### Monetary Value
How much the customer spends.

A combined RFM score is used to identify customers who should receive different levels of marketing and retention attention.

---

## 💰 Customer Profitability Model

Customer profitability is evaluated beyond revenue.

The contribution framework used in the project is:

**Contribution = Gross Profit − Service Cost − Acquisition Cost − Return Cost**

This allows the analysis to distinguish between:

- High-revenue customers
- High-profitability customers
- Low-contribution customers
- Unprofitable customers

This is important because a customer generating high revenue may not necessarily generate high economic value.

## 📢 Acquisition Channel Analysis

Customer economics are compared across multiple acquisition channels:

- Organic
- Paid Social
- Search
- Referral
- Marketplace

The analysis focuses on **contribution rather than customer volume alone**.

A channel that generates many customers may still be unattractive if its acquisition cost significantly reduces customer contribution.

---

## 🧮 Customer Lifetime Value

A contribution-based LTV framework is used:

**LTV ≈ AOV × Purchase Frequency × Contribution Margin × Expected Customer Life**

LTV is considered together with:

- CAC
- Payback period
- Retention
- Repeat purchase
- Contribution margin

This helps determine whether customer acquisition can create sustainable long-term value.

---

## 🛍️ AOV & Purchase Frequency Strategy

Two major customer-growth levers are analyzed:

### Increasing AOV

Possible strategies:

- Product bundles
- Cross-selling
- Premium products
- Minimum-order thresholds
- Personalized recommendations

### Increasing Frequency

Possible strategies:

- Replenishment reminders
- Subscriptions
- Loyalty programs
- Personalized CRM
- Repeat-purchase incentives

The appropriate strategy depends on the customer's segment and profitability profile.

---

## 🏷️ Discount Sensitivity

The project evaluates the relationship between discounting and customer profitability.

Excessive discounting can:

- Reduce realized revenue
- Reduce contribution margin
- Increase promotional dependency
- Train customers to wait for discounts

Therefore, the project recommends **segment-specific offers instead of blanket discounting**.

---

## 🔄 Retention Strategy

A differentiated retention framework is developed for each segment.

### VIP
Personalized and premium retention.

### High Value
Cross-selling and loyalty development.

### Emerging
Frequency-building and category expansion.

### At Risk / Low Value
Automated and low-cost win-back programs.

The objective is to maximize **incremental customer contribution**, rather than simply increasing campaign response rates.

---

## 🔁 Win-Back Strategy

Customers with:

- High historical spend
- Strong previous purchase frequency
- Increasing recency

can become priority win-back candidates.

Win-back campaigns should be evaluated using:

- Reactivation rate
- Incremental revenue
- Incremental contribution
- Second purchase after reactivation
- Cost per reactivated customer

---

## 🏙️ City-Level Customer Economics

Customer profitability is also analyzed across eight modeled Indian cities:

- Delhi NCR
- Mumbai
- Bengaluru
- Hyderabad
- Pune
- Chennai
- Kolkata
- Ahmedabad

City-level analysis helps identify markets where additional customer acquisition or retention investment may generate stronger economic value.

---

## ⚠️ Profitability Risks

Key risks evaluated include:

### Over-Discounting
Revenue growth without contribution growth.

### High Acquisition Cost
Increasing CAC can make customer acquisition economically unattractive.

### Return Costs
High return rates can significantly reduce contribution.

### VIP Concentration
Losing a small number of high-value customers can have a material impact.

### Data Quality
Poor customer identification can distort segmentation and LTV calculations.
